Prompt design can itself be treated as a learning problem. Instead of writing one prompt by intuition, define a candidate space, a performance measure, and a strategy that searches for better candidates.

<h2 id="prompt-optimization">Prompt optimization</h2>

Discrete prompt optimization searches over human-readable instructions or demonstrations. A model may propose variants; an evaluator scores them on a development set; search retains or mutates the strongest candidates.

Three ingredients determine whether this is useful:

- The **search space** must contain meaningful alternatives.
- The **metric** must reflect the deployed objective rather than a convenient proxy.
- The **evaluation set** must be large and varied enough to discourage overfitting.

Optimized prompts should still be inspected. A candidate can exploit quirks in the judge or dataset while becoming less robust elsewhere.

<h2 id="soft-prompts">Soft prompts</h2>

A soft prompt is a learned sequence of continuous vectors prepended to the model input. Gradient descent tunes these vectors while the base model can remain frozen. Related parameter-efficient methods learn small adapters or low-rank updates.

Soft prompts can specialize behavior with few trainable parameters, but they are not naturally readable. They also depend on a particular model and embedding space, unlike a text instruction that can be moved between systems.

<h2 id="prompt-compression">Prompt compression</h2>

Long prompts increase latency, attention cost, and cache memory. Compression removes redundant demonstrations, summarizes context, or selects only the passages most relevant to the current query.

Compression is successful when it preserves **task-relevant information**, not when it merely minimizes token count. Evaluate the complete pipeline: a cheap compressed prompt that frequently omits decisive evidence is not efficient in practice.

> Prompt learning shifts effort from manually choosing words to designing an objective and evaluation loop. The quality of that loop becomes the limiting factor.
