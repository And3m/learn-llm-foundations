Self-supervised tasks turn plain text into a vast labeled dataset. The trick is to split information the model can see from information it must predict. Different splits encourage different capabilities.

<h2 id="labels-from-text">Labels from text</h2>

Suppose a sequence is $x = (x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_m)$. A causal language model predicts the next token from everything before it:

$$
\Pr_\theta(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{m} \Pr_\theta(x_i \mid x_1, \ldots, x_{i-1})
$$

No annotator is needed. Each observed token becomes the target for its preceding context. A masked objective instead selects positions $M$, corrupts those tokens, and asks the model to recover the originals:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MLM}} = -\sum_{i \in M} \log \Pr_\theta(x_i \mid x_{\setminus M})
$$

The choice is not cosmetic. It determines what information the network may use during learning and which outputs it naturally produces afterward.

<h2 id="three-objectives">Three objective families</h2>

### Decoder-only: continue from the left

A decoder-only Transformer uses a causal attention mask. Position $i$ cannot inspect positions to its right, so the training setup matches generation: predict the next token, append it, and repeat. GPT-style language models follow this pattern.

### Encoder-only: fill in the missing evidence

An encoder can inspect both sides of a position. Masked language modeling hides selected tokens and asks the network to recover them using bidirectional context. The resulting vectors are especially useful when an application needs to understand a complete input rather than generate a continuation.

### Encoder-decoder: transform one sequence into another

An encoder reads a complete, often corrupted input. A decoder then generates a target sequence autoregressively. Span corruption—remove a contiguous phrase and generate it back—teaches both understanding and conditional generation.

<h2 id="choosing-an-objective">Choosing an objective</h2>

No objective dominates every application.

| Goal | Natural starting point | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open-ended generation | Decoder-only | Training and inference both predict the next token |
| Classification or retrieval | Encoder-only | Every representation can use both left and right context |
| Translation or rewriting | Encoder-decoder | The input and generated output have distinct roles |

Modern systems blur these lines through prompting and fine-tuning, but the pre-training objective still creates an architectural bias. It affects memory use, inference behavior, and how easily the model adapts to a particular kind of task.

The objective also shapes efficiency. A masked encoder predicts only chosen positions but processes the full sequence in parallel. A decoder supplies a target at almost every position, but generation later proceeds one token at a time.
