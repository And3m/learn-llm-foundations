An LLM begins with a simple objective—predict the next token—but becomes useful through a coordinated pipeline of data design, scalable optimization, adaptation, alignment, and inference.

<h2 id="mental-model">The chapter in one mental model</h2>

$$
\text{curated tokens} + \text{model capacity} + \text{compute}
\longrightarrow \text{pre-trained generator}
$$

The chain-rule objective makes every document a sequence of training targets. Scale improves the model only when the corpus, capacity, and system grow in a productive balance.

<h2 id="tradeoffs">Core trade-offs</h2>

| Design choice | What it buys | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More parameters | Greater representational capacity | Training and serving memory |
| More high-quality tokens | Broader evidence and coverage | Collection, filtering, and compute |
| Longer context | More evidence per request | Attention and cache cost |
| Sparse or shared attention | Cheaper long sequences | Restricted or approximate interaction |

Scaling laws help forecast smooth trends; evaluation must still test capabilities, risks, and the actual deployment distribution.
