Alignment adds targeted behavioral signals after broad pre-training. Demonstrations teach a basic response policy; comparisons express relative preferences; reward or direct objectives push the model toward preferred behavior.

<h2 id="mental-model">The chapter in one mental model</h2>

$$
\text{pre-trained model}
\xrightarrow{\text{demonstrations}} \text{instruction policy}
\xrightarrow{\text{preferences}} \text{aligned policy}
$$

The second arrow may pass through a reward model and reinforcement learning, or use a direct preference objective.

<h2 id="method-guide">Method guide</h2>

| Signal | Common method | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ideal responses | SFT | Expensive demonstrations; imitation only |
| Chosen vs. rejected | RLHF | Reward-model and RL complexity |
| Chosen vs. rejected | DPO | Sensitive to data and reference policy |
| Critiques or principles | Synthetic feedback | Can reproduce evaluator blind spots |
| Verifiable intermediate steps | Process/inference-time alignment | Additional labels or serving compute |

Alignment objectives are surrogates. Independent evaluation, disagreement analysis, and monitoring remain part of the method.
